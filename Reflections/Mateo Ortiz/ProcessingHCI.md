# Processing HCI

The goal of Lean UX technique is to obtain feedback as early as possible, to make quick decisions on design, this involves a greater level of collaboration with the team. <mark>The key points of Lean UX are: make hypotheses, mínimum viable product and quick user research and testing.</mark> Considering the key points, I believe that our team process can be considered Lean, or partly lean, because we make assumptions, given the limited data collected and the small population (students from our faculty involved in social projects), then <mark>we are forced to make hypotheses about our users. Furthermore, due to deadlines, we work with a minimum viable product (MVP)</mark>.This way, if something isn't great, we discard it for the next delivery.

For our team to be considered Lean, <mark>there should be commitment and a higher level of collaboration. </mark> Additionally, in terms of roles, there should be members in charge of leading agile development, while others should learn about UX; in terms of activities, there should be daily usability tests, weekly usability testing, and rapid prototyping (mockups, figma, etc.), to name a few; in terms of tasks, make hypotheses and measure success metrics for each sprint. That said, <mark>the cost-benefit ratio of integrating Lean into our development could work;</mark> on the benefit side, <mark>we would constantly conduct usability tests</mark>, which reduces the risk of designing something that doesn't meet user needs; on the cost side, <mark>it would take longer due to the constant testing and deadline pressures that an agile methodology entails.</mark>
