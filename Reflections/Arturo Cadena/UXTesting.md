# UXTesting
<mark>According to Interaction Design Foundation (2016), accessibility is the concept of whether a product or service can be used by everyone—however they encounter it.</mark>

Establishing a metric for this attribute is kind of interesting, there are many that I've never heard of but can be useful. <mark>For accessibility, the Likert Scale is the best option because it measures opinions and behaviours of the users. To use it, we can ask the users to grade certain elements of the prototype like text clarity, button identification, visual contrast and more, while doing the task of starting the next lesson and adding friends from notes.</mark>

<mark>The data collected would be quantitative and it could be analyzed by getting the average and standard deviation. This will help to know which elements should be modified, kept, or removed</mark> (especially the ones who got an average of 1 or 2). 

At the beginning, I thought this attribute wasn’t relevant for UX, but <mark>after realizing and researching the amount of people with low vision, I think that it would be interesting to plan how to include it in the future.</mark> Accessibility wasn’t included for being kind of complicated measuring and for probably taking too much time, <mark>even though now it can be important for the project due to the number of people with this disability and also to give a message that everyone can learn soft skills.</mark>
