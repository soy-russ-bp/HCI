# UI vs UX
*José Luis Lara Rubio*

To really understand the different software engineering concepts we learn, a common recommendation from our teachers is to come up with our own personal definitions (without making things up). The way I understand this is: if I had to write the dictionary entry for one of these concepts, what would I write?

Before comparing UX and UI, I believe it's a good idea to do this exercise:

UX: The feelings and emotions of a user towards a product before, during, and after interacting with it.

UI: The components of a computer program that a user can see and/or interact with.

These definitions clearly highlight the difference between the two concepts: <mark>UX is the abstract idea of the user's feelings towards a product, while UI is the concrete implementation of the product with which the user interacts.</mark>

I believe that in our project, we made the mistake of focusing our efforts thinking how to design a good UI (not even implementing it) without giving much thought to the kind of experience we wanted it to create.

The two examples I can think of are the design system and accessibility guidelines we specified in our non functional requirements. <mark>Both of these are relevant to UX, but only because they influence how our UI is built.</mark> They are interesting considerations, but definitely not the main focus of the subject.
