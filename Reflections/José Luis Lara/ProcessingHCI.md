Lean UX is a technique based on making assumptions about ways we can provide value to the user (in a UX context) and specifying criteria with which we can confirm if said assumptions improve the product or not, for this, it is necessary to have a streamlined process to implement changes and receive direct feedback / data from our users [[1]](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/article/a-simple-introduction-to-lean-ux)
. Currently I don't believe any of our processes could be considered "lean", because we do not have a prototype or MVP which we could give to our users. I do not dislike the idea of a Lean approach to UX, it seems like a great way to iterate on a product, always ensuring that the changes made are what the user needs / wants, it is very similar to the process described by Manuel Pamplona on the talk he gave a while ago, which gives me some certainty that a method like it can actually be used on a real world environment to get good results.
However, I also believe that ==if we had implemented lean UX from the beginning of our project it would have hindered us gravely.== Lean UX is based on implementing small changes to your product that will bit by bit improve the final result, if your initial guess is good enough then lean UX will quickly get you to a very high quality product, but, ==if you start with a complete misunderstanding of your users (like we did) it will take you a long time before your product is actually providing value to them.==
This can be easily avoided by having a more traditional UX approach at the beginning of a project than then becomes “leaner” once we can make informed assumptions instead of blindly guessing.
