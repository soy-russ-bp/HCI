One important UX attribute that we did not include in our project (although it was originally considered) is appropriateness recognizability, defined in the ISO 25010 as "Degree to which users can recognize whether a product or system is appropriate for their needs" [[1]](https://iso25000.com/index.php/en/iso-25000-standards/iso-25010/61-interaction-capability)

To map it to our project we could define a NFR that states "The user must feel like they are learning when using  our app", this is clearly very ambiguous but by using a quick survey where the user ranks from one to five how much they think they learned on their last block of lessons we could have an objective indicator of where users feel like they are getting more value from our app and where our lessons could be improved.

This attribute is in my opinion the most important UX attribute for e-learning platforms. <mark>Users in these platforms have the explicit goal of learning, so if they feel like they are learning they will keep using the app.</mark> It is definitely something I will keep in mind when designing software products from now on.
