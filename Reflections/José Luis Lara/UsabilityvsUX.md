# Usability vs UX
*José Luis Lara Rubio*

Both UX and HCI focus on improving the interaction between people and technology, UX being a subset of HCI. UX considers the entire journey of a user with the product, including how the user feels before, during and after interacting with it, focusing on feelings and emotions. [[1]](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/human-computer-interaction-not-vs-user-experience-design-a893caa5d134)

Usability does not consider how the user perceives the product, only how efficient and effective it is for them to interact with it [[2]](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/article/the-7-factors-that-influence-user-experience), UX does, having usability as one of the attributes that contributes to it. [[3]]( https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/#iso:std:iso:9241:-11:ed-2:v1:en)

For example, in our project we could do a marketing push to educate people on the importance of softskills and the benefits they could receive by improving theirs, this would probably make users more excited about using our application, it would not change anything about the product but **`by being more excited to use it, it is likely they will have a better experience with it.`**

A feature we could add to improve UX that is not related to usability is an interactive progress tracker that allows users to see a visual representation of their progress in a course, adding prices (possibly badges) for reaching certain milestones.

This feature would be mainly focused on improving desirability [[2]](https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/article/the-7-factors-that-influence-user-experience), giving the user a sense of accomplishment and continuous goals to aim for.

The effectiveness of this feature could be tested by tracking:
- How long do users spend on the app.
- How many lessons do they do in one session.
- Does user retention increase when close to a milestone?

These metrics would have to be compared to a version of the app without the progress tracking feature to ensure that they do improve.
