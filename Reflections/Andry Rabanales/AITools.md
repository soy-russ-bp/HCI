AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude are very useful when you already have a clear idea and just need to put it into words or code. They make prototyping much faster, but they cannot decide what the right thing to build is. That responsibility is still ours as designers. AI does not know the needs of Sofia or José; it can only give general advice that might apply to anyone. For this reason, I believe AI helps productivity but cannot replace the understanding that comes from real user research. It is a great assistant, but not a substitute for UX thinking.

As I mentioned on my ProcessingHCI reflection, going in blindly when you start prototyping is a very dangerous practice that can lead to building a product that no one gets value from. AI does not know who your users are, you have to find that out yourself. And once you do, you also have to figure out how to translate the information into attributes that will benefit them. In my experience, AI usually gives general advice that applies to anyone but not specifically to our project.
