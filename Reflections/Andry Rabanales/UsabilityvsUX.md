# Usability vs UX
For HCI, I also reflected on Usability and User Experience (UX) and how they connect to the bigger field of Human-Computer Interaction. HCI is the main study of how people use computers. Usability is one important part of HCI, it's about making sure the product is easy to use. Like, can users find the buttons? Can they finish tasks without getting confused? It covers things like if the app is easy to learn and easy to control. But UX is bigger than just usability. UX is about the user's total feeling about the product. This includes if it's easy to use, but also if it looks nice, if it makes the user feel happy or confident, and if it's actually useful for their life. So, <mark>usability is about function, while UX is about the feeling and the whole experience.</mark> For our soft skills project, usability means asking: Can Sofia easily navigate the lessons? Can José understand his progress chart? But UX means asking: Does the app make José feel more confident about his skills? Does Sofia feel like the app is preparing her for a real job? To improve the UX, maybe we could add a feature like "Friendly Feedback Notes". After a user finishes an exercise, instead of just a score, the app could show a nice, encouraging message related to their goals, like telling José "Good job practicing!" This feature focuses on making the user feel good (UX), not just making the app easy to use (Usability). We could check if users like this by asking them how it makes them feel during testing.
