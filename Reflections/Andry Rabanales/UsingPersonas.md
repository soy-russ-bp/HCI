# Elicitation Methods
Elicitation methods are the ways we gather information from users when starting a project. In our HCI project, a main way we seemed to gather and represent user information was through creating personas. Using personas is a technique where we create fictional characters based on research about real users. These characters, like our Sofia and José, help the whole team understand who we are building the product for. They show the users' goals, their problems (like Sofia finding current tools not practical, or José feeling insecure), and what they need from our soft skills app. <mark>Our team made two detailed personas with pictures, goals, and learning preferences, which is good. It shows we tried to understand potential students.</mark> The outcome of creating Sofia and José is helpful because it shows two different user types we need to think about. But, maybe the way we presented them could be improved by adding how we got the information – did we do interviews or surveys? We could also add short stories about how Sofia or José might use our app. Reflecting on the process, making personas was useful. For next time, <mark>maybe we could plan to use different methods together, like surveys and interviews, to get richer information.</mark> We should also make sure our personas are based on real data and are specific enough to help us make design decisions; it's always good practice to make sure requirements are clear and correct.
