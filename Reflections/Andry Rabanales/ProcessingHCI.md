# Processing HCI
I learned about Lean UX for our HCI class, and it seems like a useful way to build products. The main idea is that the team should work closely together and build things fast in small pieces. Then, we should test these small pieces with real users right away to learn if our ideas are good. Lean UX focuses on getting good results for the user, not just making lots of features. It also says we should talk to users often to understand what they really need and try not to waste time on extra things like too much paperwork if it doesn't help. Looking at our team's project, we did make the personas, Sofia and José, which is good because it shows we are thinking about our users. We also thought about how easy the app should be to use when planning. However, I'm not sure if our whole process was really Lean UX. <mark>Maybe we planned too much at the beginning instead of testing small ideas quickly.</mark> To be more Lean, our team would need to work together even more, make simple versions of features just for testing, and show them to users much earlier. I think this effort would be worth it for our soft skills app. <mark>It's hard to know the best way to teach these skills, so testing small ideas often would help us learn faster and build something that really helps students</mark> like Sofia and José, instead of wasting time on features they don't like or need.
