UX testing goes beyond verifying if an application functions correctly; it evaluates whether the user perceives value in every interaction. One critical attribute is appropriateness recognizability, defined in ISO 25010 as the degree to which users can recognize that the system meets their needs. In learning apps, this means that students must feel they are actually learning and progressing.

To measure this, we designed surveys that asked students to rate their perceived learning on a scale of 1 to 5 after each lesson block. This complements usability metrics such as task completion rates and error counts, providing a more complete picture of engagement and motivation. Think-aloud protocols were also conducted, observing how users navigated lessons in real-time and noting moments of hesitation, confusion, or frustration.

For example, José hesitated when choosing optional exercises, indicating unclear instructions or interface elements. Analyzing these moments informed iterative design changes, such as improved labeling, contextual hints, and dynamic progress feedback. UX testing thus becomes a multi-dimensional process: it captures behavioral data, emotional response, and perceived value, allowing continuous improvement of both the product and the learning experience.
