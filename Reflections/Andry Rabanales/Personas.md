Personas are essential for guiding design decisions in a user-centered way. In our project, we created detailed personas representing our target users, including Sofia and José, to capture their goals, motivations, and pain points. Sofia’s persona focuses on career advancement—she wants to develop leadership, communication, and networking skills that are directly applicable in real-world professional environments. She values interactive exercises, gamified modules, and clear indicators of progress, as these help her measure growth and maintain motivation.

José’s persona emphasizes confidence-building and teamwork skills. He prefers self-paced learning and clear, step-by-step instructions that reduce anxiety and allow him to gradually develop competence. He benefits from encouraging feedback, visual progress trackers, and milestones that make his learning achievements visible and rewarding.

Using these personas informed multiple design decisions. For example, lesson sequences were structured differently for each persona: Sofia’s path includes optional networking modules, while José’s path focuses on confidence-building exercises. This allowed us to prioritize features based on real user needs, rather than assumptions. Furthermore, personas helped us define metrics for success: completion rates, engagement levels, and emotional responses are tracked for each persona type to ensure the app delivers meaningful learning experiences.

In conclusion, personas are not just representations of users; they are tools to align design with user goals, reduce the risk of irrelevant features, and ensure that every interaction supports meaningful outcomes. For our soft skills app, Sofia and José serve as ongoing references for evaluating usability, UX, and feature relevance throughout the development cycle. By continuously considering their perspectives, we can create a platform that is both effective and engaging, maximizing learning impact and user satisfaction.
