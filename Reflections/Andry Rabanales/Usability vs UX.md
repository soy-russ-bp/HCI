Usability and UX are often used interchangeably, but they serve different purposes. Usability measures efficiency, effectiveness, and error prevention: can a user complete a task quickly, accurately, and with minimal frustration? UX measures the entire journey, including emotions, motivation, and perceived value. Both are critical, but UX encompasses a broader scope.

In our learning app, usability is demonstrated when Sofia can navigate lessons without confusion, complete exercises, and understand system feedback. Metrics for usability include error rates, task completion times, and misclick frequency. UX, however, evaluates whether José feels confident after completing a module, whether progress indicators motivate him to continue, and whether the system’s design supports his overall learning goals.

We also incorporated motivational design elements that enhance UX without altering usability. For instance, a progress tracker with badges rewards consistent performance, provides tangible feedback, and enhances engagement. Users gain a sense of achievement, which influences retention and long-term learning. Measuring UX requires both quantitative and qualitative methods: time-on-task and completion rate (quantitative) combined with user surveys, interviews, and think-aloud protocols (qualitative).

Through this approach, we see that usability ensures the app works, while UX ensures it feels valuable, engaging, and meaningful. In educational apps, the emotional experience can be the difference between abandoning or completing a course. Designing for UX thus requires empathy, continuous observation, and adaptation based on user responses, ensuring that interactions are both efficient and enriching
